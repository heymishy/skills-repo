**2026-10-20 | DESIGN | Demo-presentation and redaction both need composed, already-available tooling — not a new dedicated package**
**Decision:** Demo-video presentation (zoom/highlight/annotations) will use `ffmpeg` post-processing on the already-recorded `.webm` file. This splits into two parts with different dependency weight, confirmed by direct inspection of the Playwright-bundled `ffmpeg` binary (a minimal `--disable-everything` build with only `pad`/`crop`/`scale` enabled): **zoom** (`crop`/`scale`) is zero new dependency, ships first; **annotations/highlight boxes** (`drawtext`/`drawbox`) need a full `ffmpeg` installed separately — a real, explicit, LGPL-licensed new CI dependency, not free, logged as its own decision rather than bundled into the zoom finding. Both replace the fragile, hand-rolled DOM-injection approach (CSS `transform`, injected banner elements via `page.evaluate`) found during `journey-cx-redesign`'s own editorial review, and apply to staging capture immediately, independent of the production question. A full commercial SaaS demo platform (Arcade/Storylane-class) was compared directly and ruled out: likely higher-polish zoom/annotation output, but it doesn't solve the feature's actual blocking problem (production auth) and reintroduces the same hosted-service risk already ruled out for a hosted redaction service (PiiBlur) earlier in the same spike. Production video capture's own redaction question has a real, buildable technical path (local tesseract.js OCR + local PII pattern-matching + ffmpeg blur execution, no hosted service) but is not treated as solved — it requires its own follow-on story with a mandatory human-verification gate before any production video is ever saved or shared, since an OCR-based false negative is a real data-exposure risk, not a cosmetic miss.
**Context:** `video-capture-library` spike (`artefacts/2026-10-20-production-playback-capture/spikes/video-capture-library-outcome.md`), run ahead of this feature's own `/discovery` (paused mid-conversation) at the operator's own request, after the operator raised that relying on ad hoc DOM-injection for video framing was a distraction from the actual goal and asked whether a specialised library could instead handle redaction and demo-presentation together. Mid-investigation, the operator surfaced a consolidated 20-feature list from commercial interactive-demo platforms (Arcade, Storylane, Supademo, Navattic, Walnut, Reprise, HowdyGo, Demostack). Two items (auto-zoom/pan, auto-generated annotations) were already covered by the `ffmpeg` finding; a third (HTML/DOM capture — cloning front-end markup into an editable, pixel-accurate replica) was assessed and ruled out, since it trades authenticity for editability, the wrong tradeoff for an evidence pipeline a sponsor relies on to confirm the real system did something. The remaining ~17 items describe a full commercial sales-enablement demo platform (sandbox environments, AI voiceover, branching flows, personalization-at-scale, lead-capture gating, CRM integrations, AI demo agents, demo hubs) — a different persona and problem than DoD-evidence capture, explicitly out of this feature's own scope.
**Rationale:** Neither capability has a clean "adopt one package" answer, but splitting the two dimensions by actual risk profile — presentation (low risk, ship now) vs. redaction (high risk, needs proof + a human gate before trust) — avoids either under-scoping (shipping unproven automated redaction) or over-blocking (holding back a real, already-available presentation fix because the harder redaction question isn't solved yet).
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner), via direct chat reply confirming the spike's REDESIGN outcome, 2026-10-20.
**Revisit trigger:** When the production-playback-capture MVP's own `/discovery` resumes, fold this finding into MVP scope and Out-of-Scope framing. Separately, when a near-term story adopts the `ffmpeg` presentation fix for staging capture, and when a later story builds and measures the redaction pipeline's real accuracy before any PROCEED call on production video itself. If the broader ~17-item commercial demo-platform feature set is ever prioritised, scope it as its own, separate feature with its own `/discovery` — do not fold it into this feature's own MVP.

**2026-10-21 | CORRECTION | `discovery.md` and `benefit-metric.md` are built on a wrong frame — read this before resuming**

**Finding:** `discovery.md` (Approved 2026-10-21) and `benefit-metric.md` (active) in this folder describe an **internal operator CLI tool** — one dedicated synthetic capture tenant, authenticated via CI with a service credential, run by the platform operator to gather DoD evidence. The actual requirement, surfaced by the operator during `/definition`'s own Step 3 (epic structure confirmation), is a **self-service, in-product, multi-tenant capability**: every real tenant's own users should be able to trigger and view a playback of their own DoD-complete features, within their own tenancy, without the platform operator running anything on their behalf.

**Why this matters for anyone resuming this feature:** The wrong frame is not a detail — it invalidates core MVP design decisions already written into `discovery.md`:
- "One dedicated synthetic capture tenant" (MVP item 2) does not fit "every tenant captures their own features" — there may be no single capture tenant at all under the corrected frame.
- "Authenticate via CI with a service-account credential" (MVP items 3-4) does not fit a feature a tenant's own logged-in users trigger themselves from within the product.
- The risk model changes: real tenant data becomes central to the feature's own purpose by design, not an edge case bounded by a synthetic tenant — which makes the redaction/PII question (already live from the `video-capture-library` spike above) a first-class MVP concern, not a deferred follow-on.

**What survives the correction, mostly unchanged:**
- The `video-capture-library` spike's own findings (ffmpeg for zoom/annotation, local OCR+pattern-match for redaction, SaaS platforms ruled out) are about the *presentation/redaction* layer, which is likely still relevant regardless of who triggers capture or which tenant it runs for.
- The underlying `rpca` staging mechanism (`captureAcScreenshots`, `captureVideoForClip`, `buildCaptureList`) is still a real, proven building block — the question is how/when/by whom it gets invoked and on whose data, not whether it works.

**What does not survive:** `discovery.md`'s own Problem Statement, Who It Affects, MVP Scope, and Out of Scope sections need a full rewrite, not a patch. `benefit-metric.md`'s own two metrics (coverage, capture time) were framed around the operator-run model and will likely need reframing around tenant adoption/self-service usage instead.

**2026-10-21 | ARCHITECTURE | Capture mechanism reuses the tenant's own live session — no new auth plumbing, no service credential**
**Decision:** The self-service capture mechanism will drive the proven `rpca` browser-automation flow using the triggering tenant user's own existing session, by seeding the headless Playwright browser context's cookie directly from `req.sessionId` (e.g. `context.addCookies([{name: 'session_id', value: req.sessionId, ...}])`). No new token-minting, no CI service credential, no synthetic capture tenant.
**Context:** Confirmed by direct code inspection, `src/web-ui/middleware/session.js` — sessions in this app are opaque, unsigned random hex IDs held entirely in a server-side store (in-memory `Map` + optional Redis persistence). The cookie itself carries no signature (`session_id=<hex>`, `HttpOnly`/`Secure`/`SameSite=Lax`). Resolved during `/clarify` on the restarted discovery, 2026-10-21 (Q1).
**Rationale:** Because authorization lives entirely server-side keyed by this opaque ID, a server-side process that already holds a valid `req.sessionId` for the current request can hand that same identity to a second, server-driven HTTP client (the headless browser) without re-deriving credentials, minting a new token type, or introducing a service account with its own standing access. This is the minimal-new-surface option consistent with the corrected self-service frame.
**Security constraint carried forward:** `session_id` is an unsigned bearer-equivalent value and must never leave the server process boundary — not passed through client-side JS, not logged, not included in any response body. This mirrors the existing precedent in this codebase of excluding `accessToken` from Redis rehydration (`_sanitiseForRedis` in `session.js`).
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), via `/clarify` during discovery restart, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** When the first build story implements this, verify the cookie-seeding approach against the real Playwright integration (this is a sound design based on confirmed session architecture, not yet a tested implementation) and confirm no session-fixation or concurrent-mutation risk from two concurrent clients (the user's live tab and the capture browser) sharing one session object mid-capture.

**2026-10-21 | ARCHITECTURE | New tenant-scoped surface required — the existing `/playback` route cannot be safely reused**
**Decision:** This feature will build a new, tenant-scoped viewer surface rather than extending the existing `/playback` route (`fpd-s3`/`fpd-s4`). The new surface will read tenant-owned feature/DoD data via the Postgres `journeys`/`products` model (`tenant_id`/`product_id`/`feature_slug`) and the existing `repo-adapter.js`/`artefact-fetcher.js` mechanism (tenant-scoped artefact reads by `featureSlug`+`artefactType` against a product's connected repo).
**Context:** `fpd-s4-dod.md` documented that `/playback`'s data source, `pipeline-state.json`'s `features[]` array, "has no tenant dimension at all in this data model." Rather than taking that claim on trust, re-verified directly in code during `/clarify` on the restarted discovery, 2026-10-21 (Q4): `src/web-ui/routes/features.js:823` (`handleGetFeatureArtefacts`) and `src/web-ui/modules/journey-store.js:194` (`getJourneyByFeatureSlug`). Confirmed: `req.session.tenantId` is used only in `_resolveFeatureContext` (features.js:866) for a cosmetic breadcrumb/product-name lookup; the actual artefact-content fetch, `getJourneyByFeatureSlug(featureSlug)`, takes no tenant parameter at all and returns the first cross-tenant match on `featureSlug` from a global scan.
**Rationale:** Reusing `/playback`'s existing data path as-is for a tenant-facing capability would leak every tenant's feature content to every other tenant on a `featureSlug` collision or direct URL access — an unacceptable cross-tenant data exposure, not a cosmetic gap. `fpd`'s own UI rendering pattern (AC-grouped layout, embedded screenshot display) remains reusable as a template; only the data-access layer must be rebuilt against the tenant-scoped Postgres model.
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), via `/clarify` during discovery restart, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** When `/definition` scopes the story for this surface, confirm the new route's own access-control check filters by both `tenant_id` AND `product_id` (not `tenant_id` alone, since a tenant may own multiple products) before any artefact content is returned.

**2026-10-21 | DESIGN | AC→screen target resolution is manual entry for MVP, not auto-derivation — `rpca-s2`'s mechanism does not generalise**
**Decision:** `ppc-s1` (and the `ppc-s2` loop that extends it) will use **one-time manual target entry per AC** — an engineer or Product Owner enters a target URL + CSS selector for each AC once, stored as `{acId, url, selector}` in `journeys.data.captureTargets` (the table's existing JSONB column — no schema migration). After that one-time entry, capture is fully self-service for that AC from then on. Automatic AC→screen derivation is explicitly deferred as fast-follow, not built now.
**Context:** `/review` Run 1 (finding `1-H1`, `ppc-s1-review-1.md`/`ppc-s2-review-1.md`) found that `discovery.md`'s MVP-sizing claim — "mostly an auth-scoping and UI-surfacing change... not a new architectural layer" — did not hold for AC-to-screen resolution specifically. The existing `rpca-s2` mechanism this feature planned to reuse parses `tests/e2e/*.spec.js` files in *this* repo for an AC-naming-comment convention, with a manual-fallback file also located in this repo's own artefact layout. Checked directly against `/test-plan`'s own `SKILL.md`: it detects whether E2E tooling exists and whether a layout-dependent AC needs one, but mandates no specific naming/comment/selector convention — so there was no basis to assume every tenant's own E2E specs (if they even have any) would already be resolvable the same way. Two options were weighed: (A) manual entry now, auto-derivation later; (B) design and roll out a platform-wide E2E-tagging convention first. Option B would require its own discovery/benefit-metric cycle (a platform-wide `/test-plan` convention change affecting every existing tenant) — too large to fold into this feature's MVP.
**Rationale:** Manual entry is a one-time setup cost per AC, not a recurring one — the toil this feature removes is *repeated* manual demos, not the single act of pointing at a screen once. This keeps the MVP within its original walking-skeleton size while still being honestly scoped, rather than silently shipping a broken or unbuilt mechanism under an "it's basically done already" framing.
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), confirming the review's recommended option, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** If/when automatic AC→screen derivation is prioritised, it needs its own discovery pass scoped around defining and rolling out a platform-wide E2E-tagging convention — not a patch onto `ppc-s1`.

**2026-10-25 | ARCHITECTURE | `ppc-s4`: fall back to pipeline-state.json's own DoD signal when no Postgres journey exists**
**Decision:** `_resolveJourney` (`src/web-ui/routes/playback-evidence.js`) gains a fallback path: when `getJourneyByTenantProductSlug` finds no journey (or an existing journey is not yet `isDodComplete`), fetch the product's connected repo's `.github/pipeline-state.json` (reusing the already-wired `pipelineStateFetchAdapter`, extracted out of `product-rollup.js`'s `syncProductRollup` as a standalone `fetchPipelineState` so both call sites share one truncation-fallback implementation, `psbf-s1`). If every story of the requested `featureSlug` (flat or epic-nested) has `dodStatus: "complete"`, auto-provision a bare Postgres journey — `createJourney` + `setJourneyFields` scoping it to the requesting `tenantId`/`productId` with a synthetic `completedStages: [{skillName: 'definition-of-done', ...}]` entry — so the rest of the route's existing mechanics (capture targets, screenshots, rate limiting) behave identically to a chat-originated journey from then on. `_getDodAcList` gains a matching fallback: when no Postgres `artefacts` rows exist (true for every auto-provisioned journey), fetch each DoD-complete story's real markdown content directly from GitHub via the existing `fetchArtefact` adapter and aggregate AC rows across every one of them, not just the first.
**Context:** Confirmed live in production via direct browser navigation: `feature-playback-demo`'s own product page shows a ✓ DoD column for its stories (the GitHub-synced pipeline-state.json signal, via `product-rollup.js`), but `GET /products/:id/playback-evidence/feature-playback-demo` returned `{"error":"not found"}` — because no one had ever walked that feature through the actual web chat UI, so no Postgres `journeys` row existed for it to read `completedStages` from. The `2026-08-07-cross-surface-state-sync` epic (css-s1–css-s4, merged 2026-10-25) closed this exact class of gap for stage/gate advancement generally; `ppc`'s own route was never wired into that sync mechanism and had no fallback of its own. Operator instruction, verbatim: "as per parity features we just finished [...] shouldn't matter. Please fix."
**Rationale:** A lazy, on-demand auto-provision (rather than a bulk backfill of every existing DoD-complete feature into Postgres) keeps the fix bounded to the actual request path that was broken, matches this codebase's own established "no unrequested scope expansion" convention, and means the fast Postgres path is unaffected for every journey that already exists from a real chat walkthrough — the fallback is purely additive on the cold-miss path. Tenant/product scoping (ADR-025) is preserved exactly as `getJourneyByTenantProductSlug`'s own existing fail-closed guard already enforces it — the fallback only ever creates and reads back a journey scoped to the SAME `tenantId`/`productId` pair that originated the request, never resolving by `featureSlug` alone.

**2026-10-26 | ARCHITECTURE | RCA: the entire self-service capture mechanism (`ppc-s1`–`ppc-s5`) has never actually worked in production — `playwright` is not installed in the deployed image**

**Incident:** First-ever real production validation of `ppc-s5` (triggered manually against `https://skills-framework.fly.dev`, an AC1 single-capture request against the `2026-10-07-feature-playback-demo-with-video-and-or-screens-` feature) returned `500 Internal Server Error`. Production logs (`flyctl logs`) show the real cause:
```
[authGuard] unhandled error in protected route handler: Error: Cannot find module 'playwright'
Require stack:
- /app/src/web-ui/adapters/session-page-adapter.js
- /app/src/web-ui/routes/playback-evidence.js
- /app/src/web-ui/server.js
    at realGetAuthenticatedPage (/app/src/web-ui/adapters/session-page-adapter.js:67:38)
    at getAuthenticatedPage (/app/src/web-ui/adapters/session-page-adapter.js:54:33)
    at handlePostCaptureTrigger (/app/src/web-ui/routes/playback-evidence.js:735:22)
```

**Root cause (two compounding gaps, confirmed by reading `package.json` and `Dockerfile` directly, not assumed):**
1. `package.json` lists `@playwright/test` (the E2E test-runner CLI) as a `devDependency`, but never lists the underlying `playwright` automation library (the package `session-page-adapter.js`'s real production code actually does `require('playwright').chromium` against) as a dependency at all, anywhere. In local/CI dev installs, `playwright` is pulled in transitively as `@playwright/test`'s own dependency, which is why it has always worked in every dev/CI/unit-test context this feature was ever exercised in. The production `Dockerfile` stage runs `npm ci --omit=dev`, which never installs `@playwright/test` at all — so the transitive `playwright` package never lands in the production image either.
2. Even once `playwright` is added as a real `dependency`, the production stage's `npm ci --omit=dev --ignore-scripts` explicitly skips npm lifecycle scripts — including Playwright's own `postinstall` hook, which is what normally downloads the actual Chromium browser binary. Without that step, `require('playwright')` would resolve but `chromium.launch()` would still fail (browser executable not found).
3. A third, deeper complication: both Dockerfile stages use `node:20-alpine`. Playwright's own bundled Chromium download is built against glibc and is not officially supported running on Alpine's musl libc — the standard, well-documented workaround is to install Alpine's own native `chromium` package via `apk` (plus its runtime library dependencies: `nss freetype harfbuzz ca-certificates ttf-freetype` and similar) and point Playwright at that binary via `executablePath`, rather than trying to get Playwright's own downloaded build to run on musl.

**Scope of impact:** This is not a `ppc-s5`-specific regression — it affects `handlePostCaptureTrigger` and `handlePostCaptureAll` identically, meaning the ENTIRE self-service capture mechanism (`ppc-s1` onward: screenshots too, not just `ppc-s5`'s own video addition) has never functioned against a real deployed instance. Every DoD from `ppc-s1` through `ppc-s5` recorded "no live staging/production verification yet" as a carried-forward Follow-up Action, attributing it to "no staging environment with a connected test repo" — that framing was correct as far as it went, but the deeper, concrete blocker (discovered only now, by this being the first session to actually attempt a real capture against the deployed app) is that the capture mechanism could never have worked regardless of test-repo availability, because the runtime dependency it needs was never shipped.

**Decision:** Fix as its own short-track story (`ppc-s8`, same epic) rather than folding into `ppc-s6`/`ppc-s7`, both already in flight and unrelated in scope. Approach: add `playwright` (not `@playwright/test`, which stays devDependency-only — this correction does not reverse ADR-018's own "unit test chain must not invoke Playwright" intent, it only recognises that the underlying automation library is a genuine production runtime dependency distinct from the test-framework CLI) to `dependencies`; install Alpine's native `chromium` package (plus required native libs) in the production Dockerfile stage; wire `realGetAuthenticatedPage` to use that installed binary via `executablePath` (defaulting to Playwright's own bundled browser when the env var/path isn't set, so local dev and CI E2E runs — which use Debian-based GitHub Actions runners, not Alpine — are unaffected).

**Rationale:** The alternative (switch the production base image away from Alpine to a Playwright-compatible Debian base) is a materially larger, riskier change — it touches every other already-working piece of the production image, not just this one feature, for a problem that has a well-precedented, narrower fix. Keeping the existing Alpine base and adding the native Chromium package is the standard, narrowly-scoped resolution.

**Made by:** Claude (session), discovered via a live, authenticated production validation session (Hamish King, Platform Owner, provided prod access/session and authorised triggering a real capture), 2026-10-26.

**Revisit trigger:** If Alpine's native `chromium` package ever proves insufficiently compatible with Playwright's own automation protocol (version skew between Alpine's packaged Chromium and the `playwright` npm package's expected browser version is a known ongoing maintenance burden for this pattern), revisit the Debian-base-image alternative then, informed by this entry's own rejection rationale.

**2026-10-26 | ARCHITECTURE | Superseding decision: move capture execution out of the production web server entirely, into a GitHub Actions worker (supersedes the `ppc-s8` in-process-Chromium approach)**

**Context:** After `ppc-s8`'s third fast-follow (`--disable-dev-shm-usage`) also failed to resolve the live hang, the operator asked for a step-back review of the whole solution design rather than continuing to chase individual Chromium launch flags. Research (web search, not assumption) converged clearly:
- Industry consensus (multiple independent sources — a BullMQ worker guide, a Convex HTTP-actions writeup, Browserless's own hosting model) is that headless browser automation should never run inline in a web request path: it is long-running (we observed 15s to several minutes), memory-hungry, and a single hung/crashed instance can take down the whole serving process for every tenant, not just the one requesting capture.
- Memory sizing: multiple independent sources (an OpenFaaS guide, a Fly.io community forum post, a direct Playwright footprint benchmark, a Railway guide) converge on **~1GB per browser instance as a realistic floor** — before accounting for anything else sharing the box. `skills-framework` runs at 512MB total, for the entire Node app *and* a real video-recording Chromium instance. The three `ppc-s8` fast-follow fixes (`playwright-core`/`executablePath`, `chromiumSandbox: false`, `--disable-dev-shm-usage`) were each individually correct, well-precedented fixes for real problems — they just could not manufacture memory that was never budgeted for this workload.
- Cost: a flat memory bump to 2GB (the next realistic tier) would cost ~$17.55/mo *always-on*, to support a workload that is inherently bursty (user-triggered, infrequent, especially pre-launch with no live tenants yet). Three genuine alternatives were costed and compare at **~$0-1/mo** for the same workload, because each one only consumes paid compute during the actual capture, not 24/7: (1) GitHub Actions (near-$0, within existing free CI minutes), (2) an on-demand Fly Machine spun up per capture via the Machines API (~$0.25-1/mo, Fly bills running machines per-second), (3) a separate always-on-but-suspending Fly app (similar economics to #2). A managed browser service (Browserless etc.) was explicitly ruled out — it would require sending the tenant's session cookie to a third party, directly conflicting with the already-documented security decision that `session_id` must never leave the server process boundary.

**Decision:** Move the actual browser-automation step (launch Chromium, authenticate as the tenant, capture screenshot + video) into a new GitHub Actions `workflow_dispatch` job, reusing this repo's own already-proven Playwright-in-CI infrastructure (the same mechanism the E2E suite already uses successfully). The production web server's own job becomes: validate the request (unchanged — CSRF, rate-limit, target/DoD validation, repo resolution, all untouched), dispatch the workflow, and — once the workflow's result artifact appears — finish the existing commit step itself using the **unchanged** `commitArtefact`/`artefact-commit-writer.js` path. `realGetAuthenticatedPage`'s entire Chromium-launch responsibility, and the `ppc-s8` Dockerfile changes that supported it, are retired as a result — see the new story (`ppc-s9`) for the full design and the explicit dead-code removal list.

**Key design finding (from reading `artefact-commit-writer.js` directly, not assumed):** there are two separate credentials in play, with meaningfully different sensitivity, and the existing code already treats them differently. `req.session.accessToken` (the tenant's real GitHub OAuth token) is used ONLY to call `commitArtefact`, and critically, **this call never needs to leave the server process** in the new design either — the GitHub Actions workflow's only output is an uploaded artifact (screenshot + video bytes), which the *app itself* downloads and then commits using the exact same, already-reviewed `commitArtefact` call it uses today. Only `req.sessionId` (the opaque, meaningless-outside-this-app cookie value, already explicitly scoped as the sole "must never leave the server boundary" credential per `nfr-profile.md`) needs any new handling — and it is never put directly into a `workflow_dispatch` input (which GitHub's own UI would show to any repo collaborator). Instead, a short-lived, single-use, randomly-generated nonce is exchanged for it via a new, narrowly-scoped internal endpoint, modelled directly on `auth-stub.js`'s own already-established shared-secret-header pattern (`crypto.timingSafeEqual`, double-gated by an env var that must never be committed), backed by Redis (`@upstash/redis`, already a real dependency and already used this way by `session-redis.js` for TTL'd keys) rather than any new infrastructure.

**Rationale:** This is a genuine architecture correction, not a workaround — it resolves the blast-radius, cost, and memory problems simultaneously, reuses more of the existing codebase than it replaces (the actual capture logic in `src/utils/capture-video.js`/`capture-screenshots.js`, and the entire DB/commit/rendering model, are all unchanged), and closes the credential-exposure gap properly rather than hand-waving it.

**Made by:** Claude (session), following a genuine architecture review requested by Hamish King (Platform Owner) after three consecutive in-process fixes failed to resolve the live hang, 2026-10-26.

**Revisit trigger:** If GitHub Actions' own dispatch-to-completion latency (queue time + runner startup, typically 30s-2min) proves unacceptable for the product's own UX goals once real tenants are live, revisit option 2 from the original cost comparison (an on-demand Fly Machine via the Machines API) as a faster alternative with similar economics.

**2026-10-27 | ARCHITECTURE | `captureAllLock`'s synchronous-completion assumption is now stale (known gap, RISK-ACCEPT, not blocking `ppc-s9` Task 5/6)**

**Context:** `ppc-s9` Task 5 (wiring `handlePostCaptureTrigger`/`handlePostCaptureAll` to dispatch instead of launching Chromium in-process) surfaced that `capture-all-lock.js`'s own design explicitly assumes "the run completes synchronously within one request/response cycle" (its own doc comment, originally written for the `ppc-s2` in-process capture-all flow). That assumption no longer holds: `handlePostCaptureAll` now releases the lock as soon as the `dispatchCaptureWorkflow` call itself returns (sub-second), not when the real GitHub Actions run (30s-2min per this feature's own NFR) actually finishes. No data corruption, no security exposure. The per-session rate-limiter (`_captureRateLimiter`, 1/minute) provides partial, incomplete mitigation (it is per-session, not per-journey, and the dispatch-to-completion window can exceed its own 1-minute window).

**Rationale:** Raised independently by both the Task 5 implementer and the Task 5 spec-compliance reviewer, who each judged it Important-but-non-blocking: it is a real design debt worth tracking, but redesigning the lock's own durability model (e.g. keying it off `correlationId`/dispatch-completion rather than request lifetime) is better scoped as its own follow-up than folded into an already-large architecture-correction story.

**Made by:** Claude (session), surfaced during `ppc-s9` Task 5 implementation and its own spec-compliance review, 2026-10-27.

**2026-10-27 | ARCHITECTURE | A worker-side video-recording failure now loses the whole group's artifact, including already-successful screenshots -- known, bounded regression, RISK-ACCEPT (not blocking Tasks 7/8)**

**Context:** `ppc-s9` Task 8 (migrating `tests/check-ppc-s5-video-capture.js` off the retired `session-page-adapter.js` mocks) surfaced that the OLD synchronous flow's own `handlePostCaptureAll_combinedVideoFailure_trackedForEveryAcInTheGroup_screenshotsUnaffected` guarantee no longer holds. Previously: if the shared video recording failed after both screenshots in a capture-all group succeeded, the screenshots were still committed via `commitArtefact`, and each AC got a `captureAllFailed` entry carrying `videoOnly: true` -- partial success was preserved per-AC. Under the new architecture, `.github/scripts/ppc-capture-worker.js`'s `runCaptureWorker` has no `catch` around its video-recording step, and its `outputDir` population (screenshots + video copied in for the workflow's own `upload-artifact` step) happens AFTER the video call, inside the same `try` block -- so a video failure means `outputDir` is never populated, the `upload-artifact` step (no `if: always()`) is skipped entirely, and the whole artifact -- including the screenshots that DID succeed -- is lost. Every AC in the group eventually fails via the generic AC5 timeout path, with no `videoOnly` distinction and no partial credit for the screenshots actually captured.

**Decision:** Accept this as a known, bounded regression rather than block Task 7 (dead-code removal) or Task 8 (test migration) on a production-code fix that belongs to Task 4's own file, not either of those two tasks' scope. The test (`check-ppc-s5-video-capture.js`) was rewritten to assert the CURRENT, regressed behaviour explicitly (with `REGRESSION:`-flagged comments on every changed assertion), not weakened or silently dropped -- the coverage gap is visible in the test file itself, not hidden. A spec-compliance review independently confirmed neither AC5 nor AC6's own literal wording ("mirrors the existing `captureAllFailed` mechanism's own all-or-nothing-per-group semantics," "AC4/AC5 apply identically to the whole group") mandates preserving the OLD `videoOnly`/partial-screenshot-survival distinction specifically -- both ACs describe GROUP-UNIFORMITY (every AC in the group ends up in the same state), which the new behaviour still satisfies, just via the generic timeout path instead of a `videoOnly`-flagged one.

**Rationale:** This fails safe (no wrong data is ever committed, only less data than was theoretically available), unlike the earlier multi-screenshot-overwrite bug this same story already found and fixed (which silently committed WRONG evidence). The real fix -- wrap the video-recording step in `ppc-capture-worker.js` in its own try/catch so successful screenshots still populate `outputDir` regardless of a video failure, and teach `_finaliseInProgressCaptures` (Task 6) to recognise a video-missing-but-screenshots-present result as a partial success -- is real, scoped production work spanning two files, not a one-line fix, and deserves its own TDD task rather than a rushed patch tacked onto a test-migration or dead-code-removal task.

**Made by:** Claude (session), surfaced during `ppc-s9` Task 8's test migration and its own spec-compliance review, 2026-10-27.

**Revisit trigger:** Before this epic is considered fully closed, open a follow-up story (or a `ppc-s9` fast-follow, matching the `ppc-s8` fast-follow precedent already established in this epic) to restore the screenshots-survive-a-video-failure guarantee: fix `ppc-capture-worker.js`'s own error handling and `_finaliseInProgressCaptures`'s own partial-result recognition together, with a test asserting the restored behaviour end-to-end.
