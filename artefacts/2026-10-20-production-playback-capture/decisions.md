**2026-10-20 | DESIGN | Demo-presentation and redaction both need composed, already-available tooling — not a new dedicated package**
**Decision:** Demo-video presentation (zoom/highlight/annotations) will use `ffmpeg` post-processing on the already-recorded `.webm` file. This splits into two parts with different dependency weight, confirmed by direct inspection of the Playwright-bundled `ffmpeg` binary (a minimal `--disable-everything` build with only `pad`/`crop`/`scale` enabled): **zoom** (`crop`/`scale`) is zero new dependency, ships first; **annotations/highlight boxes** (`drawtext`/`drawbox`) need a full `ffmpeg` installed separately — a real, explicit, LGPL-licensed new CI dependency, not free, logged as its own decision rather than bundled into the zoom finding. Both replace the fragile, hand-rolled DOM-injection approach (CSS `transform`, injected banner elements via `page.evaluate`) found during `journey-cx-redesign`'s own editorial review, and apply to staging capture immediately, independent of the production question. A full commercial SaaS demo platform (Arcade/Storylane-class) was compared directly and ruled out: likely higher-polish zoom/annotation output, but it doesn't solve the feature's actual blocking problem (production auth) and reintroduces the same hosted-service risk already ruled out for a hosted redaction service (PiiBlur) earlier in the same spike. Production video capture's own redaction question has a real, buildable technical path (local tesseract.js OCR + local PII pattern-matching + ffmpeg blur execution, no hosted service) but is not treated as solved — it requires its own follow-on story with a mandatory human-verification gate before any production video is ever saved or shared, since an OCR-based false negative is a real data-exposure risk, not a cosmetic miss.
**Context:** `video-capture-library` spike (`artefacts/2026-10-20-production-playback-capture/spikes/video-capture-library-outcome.md`), run ahead of this feature's own `/discovery` (paused mid-conversation) at the operator's own request, after the operator raised that relying on ad hoc DOM-injection for video framing was a distraction from the actual goal and asked whether a specialised library could instead handle redaction and demo-presentation together. Mid-investigation, the operator surfaced a consolidated 20-feature list from commercial interactive-demo platforms (Arcade, Storylane, Supademo, Navattic, Walnut, Reprise, HowdyGo, Demostack). Two items (auto-zoom/pan, auto-generated annotations) were already covered by the `ffmpeg` finding; a third (HTML/DOM capture — cloning front-end markup into an editable, pixel-accurate replica) was assessed and ruled out, since it trades authenticity for editability, the wrong tradeoff for an evidence pipeline a sponsor relies on to confirm the real system did something. The remaining ~17 items describe a full commercial sales-enablement demo platform (sandbox environments, AI voiceover, branching flows, personalization-at-scale, lead-capture gating, CRM integrations, AI demo agents, demo hubs) — a different persona and problem than DoD-evidence capture, explicitly out of this feature's own scope.
**Rationale:** Neither capability has a clean "adopt one package" answer, but splitting the two dimensions by actual risk profile — presentation (low risk, ship now) vs. redaction (high risk, needs proof + a human gate before trust) — avoids either under-scoping (shipping unproven automated redaction) or over-blocking (holding back a real, already-available presentation fix because the harder redaction question isn't solved yet).
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner), via direct chat reply confirming the spike's REDESIGN outcome, 2026-10-20.
**Revisit trigger:** When the production-playback-capture MVP's own `/discovery` resumes, fold this finding into MVP scope and Out-of-Scope framing. Separately, when a near-term story adopts the `ffmpeg` presentation fix for staging capture, and when a later story builds and measures the redaction pipeline's real accuracy before any PROCEED call on production video itself. If the broader ~17-item commercial demo-platform feature set is ever prioritised, scope it as its own, separate feature with its own `/discovery` — do not fold it into this feature's own MVP.

**2026-10-21 | CORRECTION | `discovery.md` and `benefit-metric.md` are built on a wrong frame — read this before resuming**

**Finding:** `discovery.md` (Approved 2026-10-21) and `benefit-metric.md` (active) in this folder describe an **internal operator CLI tool** — one dedicated synthetic capture tenant, authenticated via CI with a service credential, run by the platform operator to gather DoD evidence. The actual requirement, surfaced by the operator during `/definition`'s own Step 3 (epic structure confirmation), is a **self-service, in-product, multi-tenant capability**: every real tenant's own users should be able to trigger and view a playback of their own DoD-complete features, within their own tenancy, without the platform operator running anything on their behalf.

**Why this matters for anyone resuming this feature:** The wrong frame is not a detail — it invalidates core MVP design decisions already written into `discovery.md`:
- "One dedicated synthetic capture tenant" (MVP item 2) does not fit "every tenant captures their own features" — there may be no single capture tenant at all under the corrected frame.
- "Authenticate via CI with a service-account credential" (MVP items 3-4) does not fit a feature a tenant's own logged-in users trigger themselves from within the product.
- The risk model changes: real tenant data becomes central to the feature's own purpose by design, not an edge case bounded by a synthetic tenant — which makes the redaction/PII question (already live from the `video-capture-library` spike above) a first-class MVP concern, not a deferred follow-on.

**What survives the correction, mostly unchanged:**
- The `video-capture-library` spike's own findings (ffmpeg for zoom/annotation, local OCR+pattern-match for redaction, SaaS platforms ruled out) are about the *presentation/redaction* layer, which is likely still relevant regardless of who triggers capture or which tenant it runs for.
- The underlying `rpca` staging mechanism (`captureAcScreenshots`, `captureVideoForClip`, `buildCaptureList`) is still a real, proven building block — the question is how/when/by whom it gets invoked and on whose data, not whether it works.

**What does not survive:** `discovery.md`'s own Problem Statement, Who It Affects, MVP Scope, and Out of Scope sections need a full rewrite, not a patch. `benefit-metric.md`'s own two metrics (coverage, capture time) were framed around the operator-run model and will likely need reframing around tenant adoption/self-service usage instead.

**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner), via direct chat correction during `/definition`, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** Next session — restart `/discovery` from Section 1 (Problem Statement) with the corrected frame. Do not resume from the current `discovery.md`/`benefit-metric.md` as a starting draft; they encode the wrong mechanism throughout, not just the wrong wording in a few places.

**2026-10-21 | ARCHITECTURE | Capture mechanism reuses the tenant's own live session — no new auth plumbing, no service credential**
**Decision:** The self-service capture mechanism will drive the proven `rpca` browser-automation flow using the triggering tenant user's own existing session, by seeding the headless Playwright browser context's cookie directly from `req.sessionId` (e.g. `context.addCookies([{name: 'session_id', value: req.sessionId, ...}])`). No new token-minting, no CI service credential, no synthetic capture tenant.
**Context:** Confirmed by direct code inspection, `src/web-ui/middleware/session.js` — sessions in this app are opaque, unsigned random hex IDs held entirely in a server-side store (in-memory `Map` + optional Redis persistence). The cookie itself carries no signature (`session_id=<hex>`, `HttpOnly`/`Secure`/`SameSite=Lax`). Resolved during `/clarify` on the restarted discovery, 2026-10-21 (Q1).
**Rationale:** Because authorization lives entirely server-side keyed by this opaque ID, a server-side process that already holds a valid `req.sessionId` for the current request can hand that same identity to a second, server-driven HTTP client (the headless browser) without re-deriving credentials, minting a new token type, or introducing a service account with its own standing access. This is the minimal-new-surface option consistent with the corrected self-service frame.
**Security constraint carried forward:** `session_id` is an unsigned bearer-equivalent value and must never leave the server process boundary — not passed through client-side JS, not logged, not included in any response body. This mirrors the existing precedent in this codebase of excluding `accessToken` from Redis rehydration (`_sanitiseForRedis` in `session.js`).
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), via `/clarify` during discovery restart, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** When the first build story implements this, verify the cookie-seeding approach against the real Playwright integration (this is a sound design based on confirmed session architecture, not yet a tested implementation) and confirm no session-fixation or concurrent-mutation risk from two concurrent clients (the user's live tab and the capture browser) sharing one session object mid-capture.

**2026-10-21 | ARCHITECTURE | New tenant-scoped surface required — the existing `/playback` route cannot be safely reused**
**Decision:** This feature will build a new, tenant-scoped viewer surface rather than extending the existing `/playback` route (`fpd-s3`/`fpd-s4`). The new surface will read tenant-owned feature/DoD data via the Postgres `journeys`/`products` model (`tenant_id`/`product_id`/`feature_slug`) and the existing `repo-adapter.js`/`artefact-fetcher.js` mechanism (tenant-scoped artefact reads by `featureSlug`+`artefactType` against a product's connected repo).
**Context:** `fpd-s4-dod.md` documented that `/playback`'s data source, `pipeline-state.json`'s `features[]` array, "has no tenant dimension at all in this data model." Rather than taking that claim on trust, re-verified directly in code during `/clarify` on the restarted discovery, 2026-10-21 (Q4): `src/web-ui/routes/features.js:823` (`handleGetFeatureArtefacts`) and `src/web-ui/modules/journey-store.js:194` (`getJourneyByFeatureSlug`). Confirmed: `req.session.tenantId` is used only in `_resolveFeatureContext` (features.js:866) for a cosmetic breadcrumb/product-name lookup; the actual artefact-content fetch, `getJourneyByFeatureSlug(featureSlug)`, takes no tenant parameter at all and returns the first cross-tenant match on `featureSlug` from a global scan.
**Rationale:** Reusing `/playback`'s existing data path as-is for a tenant-facing capability would leak every tenant's feature content to every other tenant on a `featureSlug` collision or direct URL access — an unacceptable cross-tenant data exposure, not a cosmetic gap. `fpd`'s own UI rendering pattern (AC-grouped layout, embedded screenshot display) remains reusable as a template; only the data-access layer must be rebuilt against the tenant-scoped Postgres model.
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), via `/clarify` during discovery restart, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** When `/definition` scopes the story for this surface, confirm the new route's own access-control check filters by both `tenant_id` AND `product_id` (not `tenant_id` alone, since a tenant may own multiple products) before any artefact content is returned.

**2026-10-21 | DESIGN | AC→screen target resolution is manual entry for MVP, not auto-derivation — `rpca-s2`'s mechanism does not generalise**
**Decision:** `ppc-s1` (and the `ppc-s2` loop that extends it) will use **one-time manual target entry per AC** — an engineer or Product Owner enters a target URL + CSS selector for each AC once, stored as `{acId, url, selector}` in `journeys.data.captureTargets` (the table's existing JSONB column — no schema migration). After that one-time entry, capture is fully self-service for that AC from then on. Automatic AC→screen derivation is explicitly deferred as fast-follow, not built now.
**Context:** `/review` Run 1 (finding `1-H1`, `ppc-s1-review-1.md`/`ppc-s2-review-1.md`) found that `discovery.md`'s MVP-sizing claim — "mostly an auth-scoping and UI-surfacing change... not a new architectural layer" — did not hold for AC-to-screen resolution specifically. The existing `rpca-s2` mechanism this feature planned to reuse parses `tests/e2e/*.spec.js` files in *this* repo for an AC-naming-comment convention, with a manual-fallback file also located in this repo's own artefact layout. Checked directly against `/test-plan`'s own `SKILL.md`: it detects whether E2E tooling exists and whether a layout-dependent AC needs one, but mandates no specific naming/comment/selector convention — so there was no basis to assume every tenant's own E2E specs (if they even have any) would already be resolvable the same way. Two options were weighed: (A) manual entry now, auto-derivation later; (B) design and roll out a platform-wide E2E-tagging convention first. Option B would require its own discovery/benefit-metric cycle (a platform-wide `/test-plan` convention change affecting every existing tenant) — too large to fold into this feature's MVP.
**Rationale:** Manual entry is a one-time setup cost per AC, not a recurring one — the toil this feature removes is *repeated* manual demos, not the single act of pointing at a screen once. This keeps the MVP within its original walking-skeleton size while still being honestly scoped, rather than silently shipping a broken or unbuilt mechanism under an "it's basically done already" framing.
**Made by:** Hamish King (Platform Owner) and Claude (session), confirming the review's recommended option, 2026-10-21.
**Revisit trigger:** If/when automatic AC→screen derivation is prioritised, it needs its own discovery pass scoped around defining and rolling out a platform-wide E2E-tagging convention — not a patch onto `ppc-s1`.
